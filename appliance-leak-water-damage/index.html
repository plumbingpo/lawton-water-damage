Dealing With Appliance Leak Water Damage in Lawton

Nearly every appliance leak we handle comes from one of three sources. When the supply hose on a washing machine splits, water flows under full pressure until somebody turns it off. Dishwashers usually leak a little at a time, at the drain hose or door seal, hidden beneath the cabinets. Behind a refrigerator, the thin tube feeding the ice maker may drip for months. No two of these call for the same cleanup.

Our Lawton crew starts by pulling the appliance out and finding where the water went. In kitchens that usually means under the cabinets and beneath the flooring. Laundry room water goes into the wall at the back of the machine and the neighboring rooms, and with an upstairs laundry, the ceiling underneath. We extract what is standing, measure what has soaked in, and set drying equipment sized to the wet area.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut the Machine DownCancel the cycle, and pull the plug only when the floor and the outlet near it are dry. When either one is wet, turn the power off at the breaker.
  2. Shut Off the Water LineBehind a washer, on the wall, are separate hot and cold valves. Dishwashers shut off under the sink. Refrigerator lines have a small valve behind or below.
  3. Soak Up Standing WaterUse towels or a mop on whatever water is within reach, which keeps it from spreading beneath flooring and cabinets. Do not vacuum unless the machine is rated for wet and dry use.
  4. Leave the Door ShutA dishwasher or washer that still holds water should stay closed. When the door opens mid-cycle, the full load of water can pour out on the floor.
  5. Document What FailedKeep the part that failed, whether a hose, a fitting or a valve, and get photos of it. Your insurer and the appliance maker may both ask to see it.

The Usual Culprits

What Causes It

  • Inlet valves that stick open, so the dishwasher or washer keeps taking in water past the fill level.
  • An overflow caused by an overloaded drum or by using too much detergent.
  • A dishwasher with a failed pump seal, a cracked drain hose or a worn door gasket.
  • Aging rubber supply hoses on a washing machine, which weaken and then split under pressure.
  • A dishwasher that was installed out of level or with a supply connection left loose.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Baseboards and Wall Bottoms

Water standing on a laundry or kitchen floor wicks up into baseboards and the bottom of the drywall. Often the worst of it is on the wall in back of the appliance, where it went unseen.

Mold Behind Appliances

Hardly anyone looks at the dark, warm space behind a dishwasher or refrigerator. Mold on the floor and wall can be fed by a slow drip in that spot well before it is noticed.

Adjoining Rooms

Water may pour from a washer hose for hours on end. That water moves beneath walls into bedrooms, closets and hallways, soaking pad and carpet far from the laundry room.

Floors and What Is Under Them

Hardwood, vinyl plank and laminate all let water slip underneath. Cupping and swelling show up in the boards, and the subfloor can stay damp long after the surface appears dry.

Coverage

Insurance and Appliance Leak Water Damage

Homeowners policies often cover sudden and accidental events, and a burst hose or a machine overflowing with no warning is that kind of event. The policy typically pays for the water damage, not for the appliance or the failed part. Gradual leaks usually fall outside coverage, so slow seepage at a dishwasher or an ice maker line makes for a tougher claim. How old the hose was and how it was maintained may also come up with some insurers. Before cleanup, take photos, save the failed part and read your policy.

What It Costs

What Drives the Price

  • Spread of the water into other rooms, and into the ceiling beneath an upstairs laundry if there is one.
  • Type of water, since gray discharge requires sanitizing and clean supply water does not.
  • Whether cabinets and flooring can be dried in place or need to be removed.
  • Presence of mold, which adds removal work in front of the drying.

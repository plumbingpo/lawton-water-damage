Wall and Drywall Water Damage Help in Lawton

Drywall acts like a wick. When water reaches the bottom of a wall, the gypsum and paper draw it upward, often a foot or more above the level the water ever reached on the floor. Insulation and the rear of the panel can be wet through while the paint out front shows nothing unusual. Looks cannot be trusted here, so every wall gets tested with a meter.

Plenty of walls that get wet can stay. Firm panels wetted by clean water are frequently saved, in some cases with the baseboard taken off and small openings drilled so air can move inside the wall. If the drywall has gone soft, crumbles, shows mold or took in dirty water, we do a flood cut, meaning a level cut over the damaged area with all material beneath it removed.

What It Looks Like

  • Staining in a horizontal band just above the baseboard
  • Damp, stale odor concentrated along a single wall
  • One wall noticeably cooler under your hand than the rest
  • Caulk lines cracking or gaps opening where trim meets the wall
  • Nail or screw heads popping out and seams showing through the paint
  • Swollen or warped baseboards with a gap opening behind them
How We Fix It

Our Wall and Drywall Water Damage Process

1

Map the Moisture

We scan each wall with a moisture meter and thermal camera and mark how high and how far the water traveled, including the opposite side and adjoining rooms.

2

Sort Save From Remove

Our call on each section, dry it or take it out, depends on water category, exposure time and the condition of the panel, and we explain it in plain terms.

3

Open Up or Air Out

For walls being saved, baseboards come off and air is directed into the cavity. For walls being removed, we make a level flood cut and take out wet insulation.

4

Drying and Reconstruction

Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on, usually 3 to 5 days, until the studs and the drywall left in place test dry. After that comes insulation, new drywall hung and finished, trim put back and paint.

Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

First hours

Wicking Begins

Water at the base of the wall soaks into the drywall edge and starts to climb. Baseboards and the bottom plate take on moisture.

Hours 24 to 48

Wall Swells

The wet line rises higher, baseboards swell and paint begins to blister. Growth is able to get going on the paper that lines the hidden side.

Day 3 to 7

Panels Lose Strength

The gypsum core turns soft and its paper starts to separate. Within the wall the insulation sags, a smell develops and mold moves over the back of the panel.

After a week

Framing Is Affected

Mold and the start of decay show up on framing that has been wet the whole time. The amount of removal and reconstruction grows considerably.

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Spread Into Adjoining Rooms

Water passes under the bottom plate into the next room or closet. Our team inspects each wet wall from both sides, plus the floor running beside it.

Growth Behind the Paint

The back of the drywall is dark, still and lined with paper, which is ideal for mold. It can cover the hidden side while the painted face looks clean.

Wet Sheathing Outside

Outside walls have sheathing past the insulation that can also get soaked. That layer dries slowly and is checked before the wall is closed.

Electrical Hazards Low Down

Water pools at the same height as most receptacles and cable runs. Leave the outlets on a damp wall alone until someone has checked the circuit.
